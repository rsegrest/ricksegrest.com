# Hyperfocus planner

"Launch a side project" is not a to-do item. Neither is "get in shape" or "learn Spanish." But that's how most to-do apps treat everything. A flat list of checkboxes with no relationship to each other and no plan for getting from here to there.

Big goals need to be broken down. Into milestones, into weekly tasks, into "what should I actually do today?" This app does that breakdown for you.

You tell it your goals. Type them in, paste them from a document, whatever. The AI breaks them down. "Launch a side project" becomes: design the landing page, set up the database, write the first feature, test with friends, deploy. Each of those steps gets scheduled into actual days. When you finish something early or fall behind, the whole plan recalibrates. Your planned work blocks show up in Google Calendar so you actually do them instead of just reading about them.

The interface is deliberately calm. No notifications. No gamification. No streaks or points or pressure. Just your goals, broken down into today's work, with a clear sense of progress. It's built for deep work, the kind of focused, uninterrupted session where real progress happens. The app gets out of your way so you can stay in flow.

Most productivity apps are either too simple (a blank list) or too complex (a project management tool designed for teams of 20). This sits in the sweet spot. Smart enough to do the planning for you, simple enough that you actually use it.

All your task data stays on your device. No cloud storage for your personal goals, no account to create, no login. What you're working on is your business, not a dataset for someone else.
